# Character Abilities

## Ability Scores

Each character will have a score ranging from 3 to 18 in each of the following abilities. A bonus or penalty is associated with each score, as shown on the table below. Each class has a Prime Requisite ability score, which must be met in order for the character to become a member of that class; also, there are required minimum scores for each character species other than Humans, as described under Character Species.

| Ability Score | Modifier |
|:-------------:|:--------:|
| 3             | -3       |
| 4-5           | -2       |
| 6-8           | -1       |
| 9-12          | +0       |
| 13-15         | +1       |
| 16-17         | +2       |
| 18            | +3       |

**Strength:** As the name implies, this ability measures the character’s raw physical power. Strength is a Prime Requisite for Fighter, Paladins. . . Apply the ability bonus or penalty for Strength to all attack and damage rolls in melee (hand to hand) combat (except when using a Finesse weapon). Note that a penalty here will not reduce damage from a successful attack below one point in any case (see the Combat section for details).

**Intelligence:** This is the ability to learn and apply knowledge. Intelligence is a Prime Requisite for Arcanists and Bards. The ability bonus for Intelligence is added to the number of languages the character starts play with; if the character has an Intelligence penalty, he or she will always at least know his or her native language.

**Wisdom:** A combination of intuition, willpower and common sense. Wisdom is a Prime Requisite for Clerics, Druids and Paladins. The Wisdom bonus or penalty may apply to some saving throws vs. magical attacks, particularly those affecting the target’s will.

**Dexterity:** This ability measures the character’s quickness and balance as well as aptitude with tools. Dexterity is a Prime Requisite for Thieves and Assassins. The Dexterity bonus or penalty is applied to all attack rolls with missile (ranged) and finesse weapons, to the character’s Armor Class value, and to the character’s Initiative die roll.

**Constitution:** A combination of general health and vitality. Apply the Constitution bonus or penalty to each hit die rolled by the character. Note that a penalty here will not reduce any hit die roll to less than 1 point.

**Charisma:** This is the ability to influence or even lead people; those with high Charisma are well-liked, or at least highly respected. Apply the Charisma bonus or penalty to reaction rolls. Also, the number of retainers and allies a character may have, and the loyalty thereof, is affected by Charisma.

## Hit Points and Hit Dice

When a character takes damage, he or she loses hit points from his or her current total. Note that this does not change the figure rolled, but rather reduces the current total; healing will restore hit points, up to but not exceeding the rolled figure.

Hit points above 0 represent minor wounds (scratches, bruises, sprains, etc) and not serious injury.  However, once a character has been reduced to 0 hit points, the character is rendered unconscious and there is a chance of serious injury or death.  Note that hit points can never drop below 0.

First level characters begin play with hit points equal to the max roll of a single hit die of the given type, plus the Constitution bonus or penalty, with a **minimum** of 1 hit point.

## Languages

All characters begin the game knowing their native language. In most campaign worlds, Humans all (or nearly all) speak the same language, often called “Common.” Each species has its own language, i.e. Elvish, Dwarvish, or Halfling, and members of the various species begin play knowing both their own language and Common (or the local Human language if it isn’t called Common).

Characters with Intelligence of 13 or higher may choose to begin the game knowing one or more languages other than those given above; the number of additional languages that may be learned is equal to the Intelligence bonus (+1, +2, or +3). Characters may choose to learn any other common languages. The GM will decide which rare or secret languages may be learned. The player may choose to leave one or more bonus language “slots” open, to be filled during play. A single language pick may be used to grant a character literacy in all languages they can speak.
