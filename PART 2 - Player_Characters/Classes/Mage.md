Mages are those who seek and use knowledge of the arcane.  Their magic is a not a matter of faith (like the [Priest](Priest.md)), but rather that of study and formulae.  Mages are the worst of all the classes at fighting; hours spent studying massive tomes of magic do not lead a character to become strong or adept with weapons.  They are the least hardy, having the least Hit Points and worst Saving Throws.  However, their long hours of study of the arcane arts grant them awesome magical power beyond the ken of most mortals.

## Detect Magic
Being sensitive to the world of magic, a Mage may determine if a person, place or thing is magical. Doing so requires [[Concentration]] and a full minute; so they cannot tell if something is magical simply by being in its presence and people tend to notice when a spellcaster is staring at them.  After 1 minute of concentration, the Mage may make a Class Skill (WIS) check against a [Target Number](Introduction.md#Task%20Target%20Numbers) set by the GM based on the obviousness of any magic present.  On a success, the character learns whether or not magic is present and its general type.

## Magecraft
Magecraft is the study of arcane (as opposed to divine) magic.

### Spellcasting Focus
As part of their apprenticeship, Mages create a device to aid in casting spells. This focus may take most any form (a staff, wand, amulet or similar), but must be easily seen, recognized and hand-held. It also takes up 1 Inventory Slot. A focus may be replaced by constructing a new one which takes 1 week in a Safe Haven and 100sp worth of materials.

For every level the Mage has, they may carve a sigil into their focus. Each sigil represents a specific spell the Mage has learned (i.e. recorded in their Spellbook). Whenever they cast one of these spells, while holding their Magical Focus, the player rolls d8s for their Invocation Dice instead of d6s. It takes 1 Day times the spell's Tier and 50sp per Day to engrave a new sigil into a focus.

Having a Magical Focus in hand does not count against needing a free hand for spell casting.

### Invocation Dice
As spellcasters, Mages have the number of invocation dice indicated on the table below, plus a number of additional dice equal to their Wisdom modifier. The Mage may choose how many dice to roll each time they attempt to cast a spell.

### Spellcasting
Mages record their spells in their spellbooks as arcane formulae. Before a spell can be cast, however, it must be prepared. The table below shows the number of spells of each level they may prepare after a full night's rest. This takes 10 uninterrupted minutes times the Rank of the spell, during which the character may take no other actions.

### Exhausting Magic
At a cost of 1 level of [[Exhaustion]], a Mage may choose to pass an [Invocation Roll](#Invocation%20Dice) that just failed. This occurs after a normal Invocation Roll, and any expended Invocation Dice are still expended and any Mishaps still occur.

### Arcane Tradition
A Mage must select an Arcane Tradition. This will determine which spells they have access to. See the rules on [Arcane Magic](1_The_Magical_Arts.md#Arcane%20Magic) for details.

## Arcane Traditions
There are many arcane traditions in the world.  A spellcaster will typically start with a spell list made of of spells from one tradition.  However, it is possible to learn other traditions through dedicated study and practice.  See [Arcane Magic](1_The_Magical_Arts.md#Arcane%20Magic) for a list of sample traditions.
### Alchemy
The study of the physical world and the magic hidden within.
### Animism
The magic of the natural and fey worlds.
### Elemental
The magic of the fundamental elements: Air, Earth, Fire, Water
### Illusion
The magic of trickery.
### Necromancy
The magic of death.
